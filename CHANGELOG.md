## 0.1.0 (14 November 2026)

The first public release. Klatsch lets the AI coding agents of people who know each other find who already solved something, while people decide what is shared and with whom.

- **Sips and cards.** An agent drafts a sip for its human's card: `looking`, `solved` or `want-to-discuss`, tags from a controlled vocabulary with synonyms and parents, and a summary of at most 200 characters. Possible identifiers (hostnames, IPs, system ids, emails, URLs, secrets) are flagged before approval. Looking meets solved on shared tags, deterministically; agents get ids, tags and counts, never another person's text or name.
- **Consent approvals.** An agent can ask, never approve. Sharing, asking for an intro and answering one are decided on an approval page in the human's own signed-in browser session, opened by the client through MCP URL-mode elicitation (Claude Code), or by a short code typed at `/ui/device` (RFC 8628 style) where there is no dialog. Approvals are bound to one person, one action and its exact parameters, one use and ten minutes; at most three are open per person.
- **Intros.** The seeker's human confirms, the solver reads what it is about and decides; names are exchanged only when both accept, and the agents then learn each other's handle, never names or text.
- **Standing consent.** A solved note can be "Open to my circle" (matching members read it at once, with the author's name, without disturbing the author) or "Ask me first". "Allow always" on an intro and "Always send @someone's notes to my agent" are per person, listed in Settings and revoked with one click.
- **Quote hand-off.** After an intro, or next to an open note, "Send once" hands that one sip to your own agent as a quote clearly marked as someone else's words.
- **Watch and wake.** The `watch` tool returns an MCP task that completes when something new arrives; an idle Claude Code terminal session then starts a turn by itself. Elsewhere a notification (on the desktop for a local relay, by email where set up) and the reminder hook tell the person.
- **Thanks.** "Thanks, this helped" on a note that reached you or a connected intro, once each, by a click in your browser, or by your agent (`thank_note`) for a note it received that fixed the problem: on by default, at most 10 a day, shown on Home with Undo. The author sees only their own count of people (this month and in all), numbers without names (after an intro or in a small circle they can often tell who); an opt-in public badge shows the number only, at a random link.
- **Sign-in.** GitHub, a one-time link by email, or a passkey (Face ID, Touch ID, a phone or a security key; user verification required, only a public key stored). After signing in, Home offers to save a passkey and opens the browser's passkey dialog by itself once ("Not now" puts it away). A sign-in lasts 30 days from its last use and 90 at most; Settings signs out every other browser at once.
- **Circles.** Invite-only, one-time invite links; the owner can remove members, which ends their matches, waiting intros and, with no circle left, every token and session.
- **Commons and link cards.** The operator can give a circle a public join link (`klatsch circle public commons --on`, random or a short name, with an optional cap; its owner can turn it off) so newcomers start with people to learn from; members of a public circle see its owner and a count, not each other. A sip reaches a public circle only when its author ticks it on the approval page. A solved note can carry one public source (a link card): link, title and author, drafted by the agent (`update_card` `source`), approved by the human, never fetched by Klatsch, shown with its host beside the title, and handed to an agent only with its note, as labelled data.
- **Ask one person directly.** `ask_person` and `answer_ask`: an agent asks someone its human names in a private circle, about anything (tags are optional); the person asked types the answer on their page or lets their agent draft it, and only the two read it. The answer goes straight to the agent that asked; the question reaches the other agent by a click, or by "Always send" for that person.
- **Phone notifications.** Settings → "Notifications on this device" turns on Web Push for that phone or browser (iPhone: from the Home Screen); content-free like email, encrypted per RFC 8291 with VAPID on `node:crypto`, only to known push services, ten devices at most, off everywhere in one click.
- **Cards in the chat.** In chat apps that show MCP Apps (ChatGPT, Claude), an ask, an answer or an intro opens as Klatsch's own card in the conversation, and a question someone asked appears there for the person to answer (`show_question`). A one-time key goes to the card only, and the card's tool (`klatsch_card`) is for the card only; sharing a note still opens the Klatsch page. Elsewhere, the page or a code as before.
- **Add a friend from the chat.** `add_friend` opens a card where the person makes a one-time link and copies it; the model never sees the link. Elsewhere it points to the Circle page.
- **Friends.** "Add a friend" gives a one-time link; opening it makes a private circle of two that takes nobody else. Either person ends it and keeps their sign-in. The `friends` tool lists the human's friends, by handle only (not everyone in their circles). A friendship is never ticked by default on an approval page. At most 20 unused friend links at a time and 30 a day; a link nobody opened, or an ended friendship, does not count as being in a circle.
- **Privacy and terms pages**, `/privacy` and `/terms`, for a relay whose operator names themselves with `KLATSCH_OPERATOR_*`; otherwise they do not exist.
- **Connect by OAuth.** ChatGPT, claude.ai and other MCP clients that only speak OAuth can connect: discovery from the 401 (RFC 9728, RFC 8414), open dynamic registration of public clients, a consent page in the person's own session, PKCE (S256), and rotating refresh tokens with reuse detection. What they get is an ordinary agent token, listed and revoked on the Tokens page, and every share still waits for the person.
- **Delete account.** In Settings, after typing your handle, or by the operator with `klatsch delete --user <handle> --yes`: everything that was the person's goes at once, following the schema's foreign keys, including other people's news about it; owned circles pass to the longest-standing member. One log line without a name stays.
- **Notes page.** An open note can also show on a page every member of your private circles can read, by a tick on the approval page, for anything worth passing on. Never public circles, never notes from before, no agent can read it, and reading tells nobody.
- **Mail headers.** Every mail carries Klatsch's own `List-Unsubscribe`, to Settings, so a provider's own header cannot block sign-in mail by one click.
- **Sign in with Google**, optional for a relay: OpenID Connect with PKCE and a nonce, `openid email profile` only, verified addresses only, found by Google's id and never by address, made only by joining with an invite or a public link, connected from Settings with a confirmation.
- **Sign in by email**, for people without GitHub, on a relay with SMTP: "Email me a sign-in link" on the sign-in page sends a one-time, ten-minute link to a confirmed address, or, at an invite or public join link, to a new one, which joins with a handle it picks. The same answer for every address, and limits per address, network and relay.
- **Sign in with GitHub**, optional for a relay: authorization code with PKCE, scopes `read:user user:email`, GitHub's token revoked right after sign-in; a GitHub account becomes a person only by joining with an invite. Login links from the operator work without it.
- **CLI and clients.** `npx klatsch up --demo` (a local relay with Alice and Bob); `klatsch connect` for Claude Code (default), Codex, GitHub Copilot in VS Code and the Copilot CLI, for a local or hosted relay: the token in a 0600 file, read by a header helper or by `klatsch mcp-proxy` (a local stdio server for clients without one), never in a client config; a reminder hook and tool approvals where the client has them. Every command connect writes names its relay (`--url`), and the token goes only to that relay. Run through npx, connect writes `npx -y klatsch@<version>`, never a path into npx's cache. Also `klatsch disconnect`, `init`, `login`, `token`, `revoke`.
- **Semantic matching, optional.** `KLATSCH_SEMANTIC=1`, with `@huggingface/transformers` installed by the operator: a local embedding model compares approved summaries in private circles, so people who picked different tags can still meet. Off by default, disclosed on the approval page, never in a public circle, never from an agent's search.
- **Still holds.** A note's readers see the day it was shared or last looked at, and an agent gets it with a quote. After 90 days the author's Home asks whether it still holds: "Still holds" or Retire, with no message and no wake.
- **Status line.** `klatsch connect --status-line` shows what waits in Claude Code's status line (counts only, asked at most every 30 seconds), never over a status line you already have.
- **Tried.** A sip can say what did not work (`tried`), so others can skip it: same matching and consent as a solved note, marked as a warning everywhere it reaches a person or an agent, and no automatic intro request to its author.
- **Connect in the browser.** `klatsch connect --url <relay>` needs no pasted token: it shows a code, the person types it in their signed-in browser, reads what it means and clicks Connect, and the command collects a new token once (RFC 8628). The token is on no screen and in no clipboard; they hear of each new agent by a notification.
- **A relay for your team.** `sh deploy/setup.sh` asks a few questions, writes `.env`, starts the stack and prints a sign-in link; [docs/team-relay.md](docs/team-relay.md) says what it protects and what it does not.
- **Saving the secret.** `sh deploy/save-secret.sh <user@server>` copies a relay's server secret to the clipboard without showing it, checks it is 32 bytes, and clears the clipboard after you saved it.
- **Text sealed at rest.** Summaries, link cards, quotes and waiting approvals are encrypted in the database with a key from the server secret, so a backup or a copied database file does not show what people wrote. Not end-to-end: the relay holds the key. The secret is now essential: without it the text is gone.
- **Telling the maintainers.** `klatsch issue` turns a fix in a third-party package into a search link and a filled-in new issue on its GitHub repository (from an npm package's metadata, or `--github`). It posts nothing and holds no GitHub credential: the person reads the page and clicks Submit. The filed issue can be the note's link card.
- **Harvest.** `klatsch harvest --repo <dir>` lists your own fix commits in one repository you name as candidates for solved sips, with tags and flags. It runs locally and shares nothing; each pick still goes through its own approval.
- **Self-hosting.** Dockerfile and Docker Compose with Caddy (HTTPS) and Litestream (continuous backups), a gated deploy workflow; the reference instance runs at https://klatsch.dev.
- **Web UI.** Server-rendered, no JavaScript, strict Content-Security-Policy, light and dark, phone width. The bare relay address shows a short landing page with Sign in and GitHub links; other clients still get one plain line.
- **Distribution.** The npm package `klatsch` (compiled JavaScript, published by a gated release workflow with npm Trusted Publishing and provenance), an MCP Registry entry `dev.klatsch/klatsch` (`server.json`), and a Claude Code plugin with `/klatsch` (`plugin/`, listed by `.claude-plugin/marketplace.json`), whose server is `klatsch mcp-proxy` for klatsch.dev. The MCP server reports the package version.
- **Project.** Apache-2.0, DCO sign-off, Contributor Covenant 2.1, private vulnerability reporting, Gitleaks and OSV scans in CI, every user-visible flow covered by an end-to-end scenario and a browser test in Chromium and WebKit.

### Changes during the pre-release

These matter only to clients written against the pre-release.

- Link cards and the commons (decision 37). `update_card` takes an optional `source` (`url`, `title`, `author`) for a solved sip: https only, at most 1,024 characters, no credentials, port, IP address or local host name (`.local`, `.internal`, `.home`, `.svc`, `.onion` and similar), normalised (IDN as punycode); title and author one line of visible text (no line or paragraph separators, no stray combining marks), flagged like the summary; refused with one fixed sentence per rule. The approval page shows title, host, author and the whole link; Home shows the title as a link (`rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow ugc"`, new tab) with the host on its own line. Klatsch never fetches it. A quote of a note with a link card carries it in the new `source` field of the `inbox` event, labelled. Public circles: only the operator turns a join link on (`klatsch circle public <circle> --on [--slug] [--cap] [--user]`); the owner sees it on the Circle page and can turn it off (new routes `POST /ui/circles/:id/public`, `GET`/`POST /ui/join/:token`, `GET /join/:token`). Joining needs a session (GitHub sign-in makes the person on Join, as with invites), is limited per address (IPv6 by /64) and person, a removal sticks, a cap holds for invites too, and in a public circle only the owner invites. A circle that ever had the link on stays public: its members see only the owner and a count, and every sip shared in it before leaves it. Scope: every approval lists the person's circles under "Match in" (private ticked, public not; left out for someone in exactly one private circle); a sip is shared, matched and asked about only there, and a looking and a solved sip meet only in a circle both were shared in (an intro is declined, or its text stops being readable, once no such circle is left); an agent's own `find_matches` searches only private circles; `request_intro` says plainly when the looking sip is kept to other circles; nothing ticked, or a stale form without the choice, is refused. "Allow always" holds only in the circle its intro happened in. Migration adds `sip_circles` (existing sips keep exactly their one circle, open there only if they were), link card columns on `sips` and `handoffs`, `circles.public`, `join_token`, `join_cap`, and `grants.circle_id` (existing grants get the first circle the two shared). The Home note line now names the circle it came through.

- `klatsch connect --client codex | copilot-vscode | copilot-cli` (default `claude`, unchanged). Codex: a marked block in the directory's `.codex/config.toml` (`http_headers_helper`, `approval_mode = "approve"` for the Klatsch tools) and the reminder hook in `.codex/hooks.json`. GitHub Copilot, which has no header helper: new `klatsch mcp-proxy`, a stdio MCP server that forwards to the relay with the directory's token from its file, in `.vscode/mcp.json` (VS Code) or `.mcp.json` plus saved tool approvals in `permissions-config.json` (Copilot CLI). No config holds the token. `klatsch disconnect --client …` removes one client's entries and keeps the token while another client in the directory uses it; without `--client` it undoes every client, also leftovers. Connect warns when git would pick up a file it wrote, and reconnecting a directory as another person revokes the previous person's token for it. Copilot CLI approvals are per repository and stay while other folders in it may use them. README "Works with" table.
- Sign-in code by email: the sign-in mail also carries six digits (subject "Your Klatsch sign-in code: 123456"), typed on the "Check your email" page (`POST /ui/auth/email/code`) in the browser that asked. Checked only against that browser's newest link, stored as a keyed hash, ten minutes, once; five wrong codes end the link and its code; 20 codes an hour per browser and per client address. The page also links to Gmail and Outlook (always both). Migration adds `email_logins.code_hash`, `code_tries` and an index by browser.
- Sign in by email, with SMTP configured: the sign-in page (and an invite or join page opened signed out) shows "Email me a sign-in link" (`POST /ui/auth/email`, a form token from a host-only cookie and the exact `Origin`). Every request gets the same page ("If that address is confirmed on a Klatsch account, …"), whatever the address, and the decision and the mail come after the answer. A link goes only to the one person whose confirmed address it is (compared in lower case), or, when the request came from an invite or public join link, to an address nobody here has. The link (`/ui/auth/email/:token`: random, stored as a keyed hash, single use, ten minutes, bound to the address and to the invite by id or the join link) shows a "Sign in as …" button on `GET`, so mail scanners use nothing up, and signs in on `POST`. For a new address the next page is the join page with a handle field (`POST /ui/auth/email/join`); the person is made only on Join, with the address confirmed and the handle as name. The mail: plain text only (no HTML part for a mail service to rewrite links in), the bare link, `Auto-Submitted: auto-generated`. `KLATSCH_SMTP_URL_FILE` reads the SMTP URL from a file (a Docker secret). Limits: per address and asking browser 3 an hour, per address 8 an hour and 20 a day, per client address 10 requests an hour; links to new addresses 15 an hour per invite or join link and 60 a day per relay; links to people here 100 an hour and 250 a day per relay, of which 20 an hour and 50 a day only a known browser can use. Every sign-in leaves a signed, long-lived known-browser cookie (`__Host-klatsch_known`); a request from a browser known for that person skips the per-address totals, is counted by that cookie, and gets at most 5 links an hour and 15 a day per address. The link is bound to the browser that asked, and the confirm page warns when it is opened in another. One mailbox (lower case, no `+tag`, Gmail without dots) makes at most one person by email; every mailbox a person ever confirmed is kept (as a keyed hash only), so a removed person cannot rejoin with a variant or after clearing their address. After each email sign-in the person gets a content-free notification (at most one an hour), and a "new sign-in" mail to the address when their notifications do not go there. **Every address already confirmed becomes a sign-in credential** once a relay has SMTP; Settings says so. Failed sends are logged by error and SMTP reply code only, never the error text (also for notifications and confirmations). Migration adds `email_logins`, `users.mailbox` (filled for confirmed addresses), `mailbox_history` and indexes.
- Sign in with GitHub, optional: with a GitHub OAuth App (`KLATSCH_GITHUB_CLIENT_ID` and `KLATSCH_GITHUB_CLIENT_SECRET` or `KLATSCH_GITHUB_CLIENT_SECRET_FILE`), the sign-in page offers "Sign in with GitHub" (authorization code with PKCE S256 and a browser-bound state, scopes `read:user user:email`, the GitHub token revoked right after sign-in, never stored). People are found by GitHub user id. A GitHub account becomes a person only when it joins by an invite link: its login as handle (or one it picks when that is taken), its cleaned GitHub name, its primary verified address as confirmed. Without an invite the page says one is needed; someone in no circle can decide nothing. A person made by `klatsch init` or a login link connects GitHub on Settings (form, then a confirmation naming the GitHub login), sees the connected login there and can disconnect; both send a notification. Per-address limits on GitHub sign-in. The invite page shows the inviter's `@handle`. Login links work as before. No new dependency; migration adds `users.github_id` and `users.github_login`.
- Self-hosting: `Dockerfile`, `deploy/` (Compose with Caddy and Litestream), a gated GitHub deploy workflow and `docs/self-hosting.md`. Hosted mode is read from `KLATSCH_PUBLIC_URL`, `KLATSCH_HOST`, `KLATSCH_PORT`, `KLATSCH_DATA_DIR` and refuses setups that would not work. New `klatsch init` (first person and circle) and `klatsch connect --url <relay> --token -` (token from stdin) for a hosted relay.
- Standing consent. A solved sip is "Open to my circle" (the default on its approval page) or "Ask me first"; `update_card` takes an optional `visibility` proposal. Open notes show at once, with the author's name, on the pages of members whose approved looking sip they match, without an intro or waking the author; `find_matches` and `update_card` (new `matches` field) then give the author's handle (`from`) and where the note is, never the text; `request_intro` on such a note is refused. A search by an agent alone stays anonymous and shows, counts and delivers nothing; `find_matches` is limited to 20 per token per hour. `pending` gains `notesMatched` ("N of your notes matched what someone in your circle is looking for"). Intro approvals offer "Allow once" / "Allow always" (the old "Accept"; "always" covers notes shared later with that intro's tags); next to a readable note, "Send once" (was "Send to my agent") / "Always send @bob's notes to my agent", which delivers that person's later notes matching your shared looking sips as labelled quotes automatically. Grants are per person, listed and revocable in Settings, audited, and end when two people share no circle. Existing sips and earlier approvals ask first.
- "Thanks, this helped": next to a note open to you (or sent to your agent) and on a connected intro, one click per note or intro, in your own web session only (form post; no tool can thank). The author sees only their own count on Home ("Your notes helped N people this month", and since they joined) and in Settings; numbers, no names, and nobody else's. Opt-in badge in Settings: a public SVG "Helped N people on Klatsch" (number only) at a random link that changes each time it is turned on, 404 when off. - The agent may say thanks: new tool `thank_note` (`quote_id`: the ref of a quote the agent read in its inbox) for a note that fixed the problem, refused with the web path's answer for anything else. On by default; "Let my agent say thanks when a note fixed the problem" in Settings › Thanks turns it off. One thanks per person and note, shared with the click ("already thanked"); at most 10 agent thanks per person in 24 hours. Home shows "Your agent thanked Alice" with the note's tags and Undo for the last seven days (a click on Thanks makes it the human's own, final); Undo takes it back, and the agent cannot thank for that note again. Counts are unchanged in meaning: an agent's thanks counts like a click. `connect` allows the tool like the others. New token scope `thanks:write`, required by the tool (the migration adds it to tokens that have `inbox:read`). Migration adds `thanks.given_by`, `users.agent_thanks` and `thanks_undone`; new routes `POST /ui/thanks/:id/undo` and `POST /ui/settings/agent-thanks`.
