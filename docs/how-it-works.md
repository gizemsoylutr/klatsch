# How Klatsch works

The words Klatsch uses, and what each step shares with whom. The rules behind them, and how they are enforced: [security model](security-model.md). Every flow, step by step, as diagrams drawn from the end-to-end tests: [flows](flows/README.md).

- **Sips:** an agent drafts a line for its human's card: a status (`looking`, `solved`, `tried`, `want-to-discuss`), tags from a controlled vocabulary, and a summary of at most 200 characters. Nothing is shared until the human approves it.
- **Tried:** a note that says what did not work, so others can skip it. It follows the same rules as a solved note (open or ask first, the same circles) and is always marked as not a fix, for the reader and for their agent. Sharing a looking sip never sends an intro request to someone for whom it did not work; you can still ask by hand.
- **Still holds:** a note shows the day it was shared or last looked at. After 90 days your Home asks whether it still holds: "Still holds" moves the date on, Retire takes it down. Nothing wakes you for this.
- **Matches:** `looking` meets `solved` on shared tags. Agents get a match id and the tags, never the other person's text or name (only a quote you choose to send them, below).
- **Semantic matching (optional):** where a relay's operator turned it on, a looking sip and a solved one in the same private circle can also match on what they say, without a shared tag. A model inside the relay does the comparing and can only make a match; the approval page says so. Off by default.
- **Open or ask first:** when you share a solved sip, you choose on the approval page. "Open to my circle" (the default): whoever it matches reads it on their Klatsch page at once, with your name, and you are not asked or disturbed; their agent learns only your handle, and you later hear how many of your notes matched what someone is looking for (never who). Only a looking sip the other human shared counts, never a search by their agent. "Ask me first": the intro flow below, where you can "Allow once" or "Allow always" for that person: they then see the notes you share from then on with that intro's tags.
- **Notifications:** turn them on in Settings for your phone or browser (on an iPhone, add Klatsch to the Home Screen first). They say only that something is new, never what anyone wrote. A chat app also checks Klatsch when you start a conversation.
- **In ChatGPT or Claude:** you approve in a Klatsch card inside the chat, and a question for you appears there too, to answer without leaving the conversation. Sharing a note still opens the Klatsch page.
- **Friends:** say "add a friend" in ChatGPT or Claude, or use "Add a friend" on the Circle page: you get a link to send by any messenger; when your friend opens it, you are friends: a private circle of two. Either of you can end it.
- **Asking one person:** tell your agent "ask @ayse …", about anything. It drafts the question, you approve it, and only Ayse reads it, with your name. She types the answer on her page, or lets her agent draft it and approves it; only you read it, and it goes straight to the agent you asked with. Her agent gets your question only when she sends it there. You need to be friends or share a private circle; the `friends` tool lists your friends by handle. A friendship is for asking: your notes are matched there only when you tick it on the approval page.
- **Notes page:** when you open a note to your circle, you can also tick "Also on the Notes page": everyone in your private circles can then read it any time, with your name, without being stuck on it. It is for anything worth passing on. Public circles never show there, notes from before stay off it, and no agent can read the page: you can send one note to your agent by a click.
- **Intros:** the agent asks; its human confirms; the other human reads what it is about and decides. Names appear only when both have accepted. For 24 hours after that, each person sees the other's sip and can send it to their own agent with "Send once": it arrives once, as a quote marked as someone else's words. After that, or once the sip is retired or either person leaves the circle, only the names stay.
- **Always send:** next to someone's note you can choose "Always send @bob's notes to my agent": from then on, Bob's notes that match a looking sip you shared reach your agent as labelled quotes without a click. Every "always" is per person, listed in Settings and revoked there with one click.
- **Your own history:** your agent also gets your own earlier sips, so it can tell you that you solved this before.
- **Starting from your past work:** `klatsch harvest --repo <dir>` lists your own fix commits in that one repository as candidates, on your machine; nothing is scanned unless you name it, and nothing leaves. You pick, your agent rewrites each pick in plain words, and each goes through the normal approval.
- **Approve in your own browser:** when your agent wants to share a sip or send or answer an intro, Claude Code asks to open a Klatsch approval page (MCP URL-mode elicitation, outside the model's reach). The page shows exactly what you approve and only opens for you. On another device, type the short code from the dialog at `/ui/device`, like signing in to GitHub on a TV (RFC 8628). Clients without dialogs get the code from the agent instead (as in CIBA).
- **Connecting an agent:** `klatsch connect --url <relay>` shows a code; you type it in your signed-in browser and click Connect, and the command collects a token by itself. Only type a code you got from your own terminal.
- **Circles:** a private circle grows by invite only. Any member can make a one-time invite link; the person who made the circle can remove members.
- **Commons (public circles):** the relay's operator can turn on a public join link for a circle (`/join/<token>`), and anyone who signs in can join until its owner turns it off; klatsch.dev has one called "commons". Every approval lists your circles under "Match in": private ones ticked, public ones not, with what ticking a public one means (strangers may read your looking sip with an intro request, or your open note at once). A sip is shared, matched and asked about only in the circles you tick; joining a circle later does not widen sips shared before. "Allow always" holds only in the circle where that intro happened. Members of a public circle see its owner and a count, not each other's names, and only the owner invites there.
- **Telling the maintainers:** when a fix was about a third-party package, `klatsch issue --package <name> --title … --body …` gives you a link that searches its issues and a link to a filled-in new issue on GitHub. Klatsch posts nothing: you read the page and click Submit. The filed issue can then be the link card of your solved note.
- **Link cards:** a solved note can carry one public source: a link, a title and the author. Your agent adds it (`source` in `update_card`) when a public post, doc or issue fixed the problem; you approve it with the note. Klatsch never opens the link; it shows the title with the site's host beside it, and the link goes to an agent only with the note, when its reader sends it there.
- **Status line:** `klatsch connect --status-line` adds what waits to Claude Code's status line: counts, never names. It never replaces a status line you already have, and `disconnect` takes it out.
- **Watch:** after sharing or asking, the agent calls `watch`. In the Claude Code terminal CLI, an idle session then starts a turn by itself when news arrives. Other clients cannot be woken; there you get a desktop notification, and your agent hears of it when you next type.

MCP tools: `update_card`, `find_matches`, `request_intro`, `respond_intro`, `ask_person`, `answer_ask`, `friends` (your human's friends, by handle), `inbox`, `pending` (for a reminder hook), `watch` (wakes an idle Claude Code session when news arrives), `thank_note` (thanks the author of a note that fixed the problem; the person can turn it off and undo it). Wire format: [`schema/`](../schema).
